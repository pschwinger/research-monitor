Rett Syndrome: the full picture for families

An honest, plain-language deep-dive into everything that matters if you love someone with Rett syndrome (with a focus on the MECP2 R255X variant): approved medicines, every avenue in trials, the genetic "cure" research, day-to-day care, and how to reach trials from Austria. Refreshed every three months, researched in six languages. Every finding links to its source.

New since the last update (25 July 2026)

  • Europe approved the first Rett medicine. Trofinetide (EU name Daybu) was authorised across the EU on 20 August 2026 for ages 5 and up. Each country still has to agree a price. France already gives it through a compassionate-access programme.
  • Both leading gene therapies have finished dosing in their decisive trials. Results are expected in 1st half 2027 (TSHA-102) and 2nd half 2027 (NGN-401). Two smaller gene-therapy trials are running in China.
  • More drug trials in Europe: fenfluramine Phase 3 is recruiting (nearest sites: Vogtareuth in Germany, Budapest, Debrecen, Kraków), and an Italian trial of mirtazapine is recruiting in four Italian cities.
  • A European family registry (rettX) is open to families in 21 countries.
  • New on this page: a section for families in Austria, a table of trials within reach, a check date on every item, source links on every finding, an update comparison and an archive of all earlier versions.
Rett syndrome comes from changes in one gene, MECP2. For decades there was only supportive care. That has genuinely changed: there is now an approved medicine, more drugs in trials, and therapies aimed at the root cause are being tested in humans. 💊 Medicines
Available now

Trofinetide (Daybu / Daybue)

First medicine approved for Rett syndrome · treats symptoms
Grounding 97%

The first medicine ever approved specifically for Rett syndrome. It eases communication and behavioural symptoms (it doesn't fix the gene). Approved in the US, Canada and, since 20 August 2026, the European Union (EMA: Daybu), for adults and children aged 5 and older.

  • Austria / Germany: the EU approval is valid here, but each country must agree price and reimbursement before it is routinely available (Rett Syndrome News). We found no announced launch date for Austria. The company said it expected a German launch in the last quarter of 2026 (company statement, not yet confirmed).
  • France already provides it through a compassionate-access programme run by the national medicines agency since December 2025: genetically confirmed Rett, age 5 or older, approved via a specialist case review (ANSM page).

Checked 28 Oct 2026 · EMA and ANSM pages read directly.

🧪 Drug trials for symptoms
In trials

Medicines being tested

Large and small trials, worldwide
Grounding 86%
  • Fenfluramine (Fintepla, UCB): Phase 3, recruiting. Already used for other epilepsies. About 200 participants aged 5-35; its main measures are behaviour and overall improvement. Sites in 10 countries (see the table below). A small open study in Madrid (8 girls with hard-to-treat epilepsy, published September 2026) saw seizures more than halved in all of them; early evidence only.
  • Mirtazapine (MirtaRett): Phase 2, recruiting in Italy. An existing antidepressant being repurposed; girls and women aged 5-39; sites in Genoa, Messina, Milan and Siena.
  • NA-921 (Biomed Industries): Phase 3. Ages 5-20, 12 weeks, placebo-controlled; sites in the US and Australia only.
  • Blarcamesine (ANAVEX 2-73): results so far are mixed. The company says a new adult Phase 3 is being prepared; no sites announced.
  • Leriglitazone (Minoryx): Phase 2a in Barcelona, recruitment finished; results were expected in 2026 but are not yet public.
  • Further away: vorinostat (Phase 1, Colombia, recruiting), NTI164 cannabis extract (Phase 2/3, Australia, not yet recruiting), paeonol and growth hormone (small studies, China).

Checked 28 Oct 2026 · ClinicalTrials.gov, EU trial register (CTIS), WHO trial search (incl. China, Japan, Australia).

🧬 Gene therapy: going after the root cause
In human trials

NGN-401 & TSHA-102

One-time therapies that deliver a working MECP2 gene
Grounding 92%

The biggest source of hope: instead of easing symptoms, these give cells a functional copy of MECP2, addressing the actual cause.

  • NGN-401 (Neurogene): children gained developmental milestones (~4.7 each; 7 of 10 gained two or more), with no loss of milestones out to 30 months. Generally well tolerated across 35 participants (August 2026). The registrational trial has dosed all 25 participants; manufacturing for an approval filing is being validated. Results expected in the 2nd half of 2027. Holds EU designations (orphan, PRIME) that can speed review. (Neurogene Q2 2026 report)
  • TSHA-102 (Taysha): delivered into the spinal fluid. Pivotal trial fully dosed (17 participants aged 6-21) plus 4 children aged 2-3. No severe treatment-related serious side effects across 33 participants (August 2026). Headline results expected in the 1st half of 2027. (Taysha Q2 2026 report)
  • China: two small gene-therapy trials with a full MECP2 copy given into the spinal fluid: GCB-002 (Genecombio, ages 2-10, by invitation) and AAV-MECP2 (Guangzhou Women and Children's Medical Center, ages 4-10, early Phase 1, no longer recruiting). No results published yet.

Investigational, not yet approved. Neither leading trial has sites in Europe.

Checked 28 Oct 2026 · company reports to the US SEC (August 2026) and ClinicalTrials.gov, read directly.

🔧 Repairing the gene itself
Lab research

Reawakening the healthy "silent" copy (X-reactivation)

Could work for any mutation, including R255X
Grounding 78%

Girls with Rett usually carry one healthy MECP2 copy that sits switched off on the inactive X chromosome. Research aims to switch that healthy copy back on, which would help regardless of the mutation. Lab work with antisense molecules against "Xist" and epigenome editing has shown strong reactivation. A company program (XGR-101) plans first human testing in 2027 (IRSF pipeline). Still preclinical.

Checked 28 Oct 2026 · no new primary results since July.

Lab research

R255X-specific: reading through the "stop" signal

Read-through drugs, engineered tRNAs, RNA editing
Grounding 78%

R255X is a "nonsense" mutation: it inserts a premature stop signal that cuts the protein short. It is one of the most common Rett mutations, so it is a big research target. The idea is to make the cell ignore that false stop:

  • Engineered tRNAs restored up to ~60% of the full protein in lab tests (Frontiers 2026). A Milan group reported restoring full-length protein in cell models carrying R255X (conference abstract, not yet peer-reviewed).
  • Read-through compounds (e.g. enhancers with low-dose gentamicin) work in cells.
  • RNA editing fixes the faulty message instead of the gene. Programs for other nonsense variants are moving closer to patients: R270X, first human trial expected 2027 (IRSF pipeline); R168X, animal studies funded in August 2026. An R255X program is listed at the discovery stage only.

Checked 28 Oct 2026 · journals, IRSF pipeline, Milan university repository (abstract only).

🩺 Managing symptoms & health (day to day)
Standard care

What to watch and treat

From the IRSF Comprehensive Care Guidelines
Grounding 92%
  • Seizures (~74% of people): anti-seizure medicines; a ketogenic diet can help hard-to-control seizures (under specialist supervision).
  • Heart rhythm (QT prolongation): an important, often-missed risk. Regular EKGs are recommended, and some medicines that prolong the QT interval need caution.
  • Breathing irregularities: breath-holding/hyperventilation; a sleep study if snoring or pauses occur.
  • Digestion: constipation (~65%) and reflux are common and treatable; some children need a feeding tube.
  • Spine (scoliosis): common, usually from ages 8-11; monitored regularly, occasionally surgery.
  • Bones: higher fracture risk; vitamin D, calcium and weight-bearing activity.

Best coordinated by a multidisciplinary team. European standards of care are being drafted by Rett Syndrome Europe (Rett Forum, November 2026). A French family survey (2026) found the biggest gaps in care coordination and the move from child to adult care.

Checked 28 Oct 2026 · IRSF guidelines; Rett Forum; French family association (AFSR).

🤝 Everyday therapies that help quality of life
Supportive

Communication, movement, music & diet

What families and therapists find helps most
Grounding 86%
  • Eye-gaze communication: eye-tracking devices (AAC) let many girls communicate choices and connect, despite limited hand use (family experiences study).
  • Physical therapy maintains mobility and prevents contractures; occupational therapy supports hand use and daily skills (systematic review).
  • Music therapy: encouraging evidence for communication, eye contact and engagement (overview).
  • Hydrotherapy & movement: swimming, treadmill and sensory approaches.
  • Diet & supplements: ketogenic diet for seizures (supervised); CBD/CBDV shows early anti-seizure promise (review). Discuss any supplement with the care team.

Checked 28 Oct 2026 · no notable new evidence since July.
